==============================================================================
INTRODUCTION                                                        *neoffice*

neoffice opens Word (.docx, .doc) and LibreOffice (.odt) documents as plain
text with Markdown-style marks, and |:w| writes them back: >

  nvim report.docx      " or :edit report.odt
<
A file that does not exist yet opens as a new, empty document, created when
you write it. Everything you can't edit as text (fonts, styles, headers,
settings, ...) is kept, and only what you change is written (see
|neoffice-safety|). It defines one command, |:Neoffice|, and no keymaps (see
|neoffice-keymaps|).

Requires Nvim 0.10+ and zlib (documents are read and written in Nvim itself,
through LuaJIT; without zlib, the `zip` and `unzip` commands). Optional: pandoc
(|neoffice-equations|), LibreOffice (`soffice`, for .doc files: converted to
.docx when opened, and back when written; a conversion is kept, by the file's
content, so it opens at once the next time, see `doc_cache` in
|neoffice-options|; and for PDFs, |neoffice-pdf|), and to show images
other than PNG (|neoffice-images|): ImageMagick, `pdftoppm`, `rsvg-convert`.

==============================================================================
THE TEXT                                                       *neoffice-text*

As in Markdown, paragraphs are separated by a blank line, and lines with no
blank line between them are one paragraph (so you can wrap long paragraphs
with |gq|: they are joined with spaces when written). Each further blank line
is an empty paragraph, which Word documents often use for spacing; typing in
one makes it a paragraph of its own, even with no blank line around it. A
line opened with |o| or |O| right next to a paragraph continues it: leave a
blank line to start a new one.

Formatting ~
                                                              *neoffice-marks*
  `**bold**`              bold
  `*italic*`              italic
  `++underline++`         underlined
  `~~struck~~`            struck through
  `==marked==`            highlighted (yellow)
  `==marked=={green}`     highlighted in another colour
  `cm^{2}`                superscript (as in LaTeX)
  `W_{food}`              subscript
  `[text](https://...)`   a link

Text typed after a word takes that word's formatting, as in a word processor.

Highlight colours are Word's: `yellow` (the default, not written), `green`,
`cyan`, `magenta`, `blue`, `red`, `darkblue`, `darkcyan`, `darkgreen`,
`darkmagenta`, `darkred`, `darkyellow`, `darkgray`, `lightgray`, `black`,
`white`; or any colour as `#rrggbb` (in a .docx it becomes the nearest of
Word's). Highlights show in their colour in the buffer. `=={color = green}`
works too; neoffice writes `=={green}` (a colour is all a highlight has).

Paragraphs ~
                                                             *neoffice-blocks*
A paragraph's kind is written at the start of its line:

  `# `, `## `, ...        heading 1, 2, ... (|:Neoffice| numbers: numbered)
  `% `                    the title (the document's Title style)
  `> `                    a quote (its Quote style)
  `- `                    a bullet list item
  `#. `                   a numbered list item (|neoffice-lists|)
  `Table: `, `Figure: `   a caption (|neoffice-captions|)

List items are indented two spaces per level, and can follow each other with
no blank line between them. Headings are found by their style,
whatever its name in your language; a heading of a level the document has no
style for gets a "heading N" style.

                                                              *neoffice-lists*
A numbered item is written `#. `, as in pandoc: the document counts. Its
level is its indentation, two spaces a level: >

  #. first item                 1. first item
  #. second item                2. second item
    #. second sub one             a. second sub one
    #. second sub two             b. second sub two
<
Its numbers are as its list's definition says (Word's numbering,
LibreOffice's list style): 1., 1.1., a), (i)..., whatever the document has,
kept as they are. A new list is numbered 1., a., i. by level (again from 1.
below those), as Word numbers one. A number at the start of a paragraph's
text ("1. Introduction", "2.1.", "49. Smith, J.") is just text: never a list
item, never escaped; `\#. ` is a paragraph starting with "#." as text. Items
one after another are one list (a sub-item always its item's, bullets under a
number too); a blank line (or a paragraph) between them starts another,
numbered from 1. A list the document numbers again partway (as LibreOffice
writes one sometimes) opens with a blank line there: delete it to number on.

                                                              *neoffice-align*
Its alignment comes after its kind:

  `<|> `                  centered
  `->> `                  right-aligned
  `<=> `                  justified
  `<<- `                  left (none of its own: its style's)

  `# <|> A centered heading`, `> <=> A justified quote`, `- ->> An item`

Alignment shows only when it is set on the paragraph itself: a style that
centers its paragraphs (some Title styles) shows no mark.

                                                           *neoffice-styles*
The styles at the top ~
A document opens with its paragraph styles at the top: those its paragraphs
have and those they are based on, each indented under the one it is based
on, so how they inherit shows. The first of each tree (the default style)
says all it gives; each under it says only what it says itself, and has the
rest from the one above it: >

  ---
  styles:
    Normal:              {font = Times New Roman, size = 11pt, first = 1cm}
      Heading 1:         {font = Arial, size = 14pt, bold, first = 0cm}
      Body Text:         {before = 9pt, after = 9pt}
        First Paragraph: {first = 0cm}
      Caption:           {size = 10pt, italic}
  ---
<
A line starting with `#` is a comment, as in YAML (`gcc` comments one out):
inactive, as if it weren't there. The lines at the top are highlighted as an
R Markdown file's YAML is, where tree-sitter has a YAML parser: each line by
itself (read together, YAML would take the indented styles as wrong, a key
with braces having nothing under it in YAML); without one, the names,
comments, and the rest in gray.

A line says `font`, `size`, `color`, `align`, `bold`, `italic`, and the format's
attributes below; a value a style says over the one above it is said too
(`first = 0cm`, `bold = no`). Editing a line changes the style itself, so
every paragraph of it follows (in the whole document: its headers and
footnotes too), as in Word and LibreOffice; and, as in Word, the styles under
it follow it where they say nothing themselves (a header's, a footnote's too,
that aren't at the top). A value taken off a style's line under another, it
has from the one above it again; taken off the first's, its default. The
indentation shows the document's inheritance: changing it changes nothing.
A paragraph keeps the format of
its own (an empty one too, which shows as a blank line and has none to say). Deleting a line, or the whole header, changes nothing; a line naming
a style the document hasn't, or an attribute that isn't one, makes the write
fail, saying so. The language server completes the styles' names (those the
document has) and their attributes, and folds the header out of the way
(|zc|). `setup({ styles = false })` doesn't show it. Two styles of one name (Word
allows it) show apart, the second with its id: `Body Text (BodyText0):`.

                                                     *neoffice-styles-reset*
`:Neoffice styles reset` makes the styles a new document's: those a new
document has (Normal, Heading 1-9, Title, Caption, ...) as it has them, the
document's defaults (font, size) too; a style it hasn't, nothing of its own,
so it follows the style it is based on. The paragraphs' own formatting (words in another font, a
paragraph's format line) isn't a style's: it stays, as do the heading numbers
(|:Neoffice| numbers) and table styles. The lines at the top show the styles
as they now are; |:w| writes them (the text isn't written again).

                                                            *neoffice-pages*
Page settings and sections ~
The document's page settings are at the top too, on a line of their own: >

  ---
  page: {size = A4, margins = 2.5cm 3cm}
  styles:
    ...
  ---
<
A section (Word's and LibreOffice's: pages with settings of their own, as a
landscape page for a wide figure) starts where a line `\newpage {...}` says,
alone on its line between paragraphs, saying what changes from the section
before, as format lines do (|neoffice-para-format|): >

  ...the paragraph before.

  \newpage {orientation = landscape, margins = 3cm 2.5cm}

  ![](wide-figure.png){width = 24cm}

  \newpage {orientation = portrait, margins = 2.5cm 3cm}

  The text goes on in portrait.
<
Attributes:

  `size`          A3, A4, A5, B5, Letter, Legal, or its sides: `21cm x 29.7cm`
  `orientation`   portrait, landscape
  `margins`       all four (`2.5cm`), or top and bottom, left and right
                (`2.5cm 3cm`); or each: `margin-top`, `margin-bottom`,
                `margin-left`, `margin-right`
  `start`         where the section starts: a new page (`page`, the default),
                an `odd` or `even` one, or on the same page (`continuous`:
                Word only)

Edit the line at the top to change the document's settings (and those of the
sections that don't say otherwise: what a line doesn't say goes on); edit a
section's line to change that section; type one to start a section there;
delete one to join its section to the one before. `\newpage {}` is a section
that changes nothing shown (it starts a new page; Word's headers or columns
may differ). Only what you change is written: the rest of a section (its
headers and footers, columns) stays as it was, and a new one gets the
section's it was cut from. In an .odt, a section is a page style
(LibreOffice's): one changed for all its sections changes it, one changed for
some is copied for those. A page break followed by text in braces is written
`\newpage \{...}`. The language server completes the attributes.
`setup({ page = false })` doesn't show them.

                                                       *neoffice-para-format*
The format of plain paragraphs (not headings, titles, quotes, list items,
captions, table cells or boxes) is what they have of their own, against
their style above. It goes on a line of its own, with only attributes in
braces, right above the paragraph: never in the paragraph's text, which
stays only text. With a `+` at its end, the line is for that paragraph and
all after it, until the next `+` line (as in LaTeX), and says only what
changes: what it doesn't say goes on from the `+` line before. Without the
`+`, it is for the paragraph right below it only (no blank line between),
over what the `+` lines give. `name = none` takes one back to the style,
and `none` alone all of them: >

  {align = justify, first = 0.5cm}+
  Small mammals budget their energy...

  {before = 6pt}
  Justified still, indented still, and this one more space above.

  Justified still; no more space above.

  {none, indent-left = 0.76cm, first = -0.76cm}+
  Alexander, R. M. (1999). Energy for animal life...

  {none}+
  From here on, as their style says.
<
  `style`                         its paragraph style, by name (see below)
  `align`                         `left`, `center`, `right`, `justify`
  `indent-left`, `indent-right`   indentation (cm, mm, in, pt)
  `first`                         the first line's indent (negative: hanging)
  `before`, `after`               the space before and after (pt...)
  `line`                          line spacing: `single`, `1.5`, `double`, a
                                  multiple, or a length (exactly that)
  `font`, `size`                  the font of its text and its size (`11pt`)
  `color`                         the colour of its text (`red`, `#1f4e79`)

A format is what differs from the style: `none` is the style's (`align =
none`: the style's alignment). Opened, the formats are shown in as few lines
as can say them, and of those the shortest, an attribute at a time: one the
paragraphs after it have too goes on a `+` line (which says only what
changes from the `+` line before it, or starts from the style with `none`
when that is shorter), one only it has on a line of its own, without `+`; a
run's alignment isn't said again because the style changes. The marks are short for alignment, on
such a line: `<|>` (centred), `->>` (right), `<=>` (justified), `<<-`
(left); `<=> {before = 6pt}+`. A line without `+` with no paragraph right
below it stops the save, and says where. Attributes in braces at the start
of a paragraph's text (`{before = 12pt} Text.`, `<|> Text.`) are read too,
and shown on a line of their own when the document is opened again (only
braces with `name = value` in them are attributes: `{this} text` is text).

                                                   *neoffice-para-style*
`style` gives a paragraph another of the document's paragraph styles, by its
name, whatever its case: `{style = First Paragraph}` on the line above it
for one, with `+` for those after it too (until a `+` line says `style =
none`, or `none`). Plain paragraphs have one of two usual styles, by where they are:
first ones (the first in the document, and right after a heading, title or
quote) the one most of those have, the others the one most of them have. In
a document made by pandoc these are First Paragraph and Body Text; in most
others both are Normal. Opened, a paragraph whose style isn't the usual one
where it is says it, on a line above it; `none` is the usual one where it is. A
paragraph you type gets the usual one where it is (not the style of the
paragraph before it, nor a caption's after a table); one whose `{style =
...}` you delete too. A style the document doesn't have stops the save, and
it says where. The language server completes the document's styles after
`style = `. To change how all first paragraphs look, give them a style
(First Paragraph) and change that style at the top (|neoffice-styles|).

                                              *neoffice-format-into-style*
On a format's line, a code action moves its attributes into the style of
the paragraphs under it: "Move this format into the style Normal". The line
at the top says them, and the format's line goes; paragraphs of that style
elsewhere that had the style's values say them now, so nothing looks any
different. It is offered only when that holds (the paragraphs under it all
of one style, saved; none that would change in a table cell or a box).

                                                              *neoffice-fonts*
A paragraph's font and size are what most of its text has, wherever that
comes from (its text, as Word and LibreOffice often set it, or its style). A
few words in another font or size say so in a span, as in pandoc: >

  The [Latin name]{font = Arial, size = 9pt} of the species.
<
Most documents have none: a span shows only text that differs from its
paragraph. Headings, lists and other paragraphs that aren't plain keep their
font as it is (unsaid), and show spans the same way. A font's name may have
spaces (`font = Times New Roman`); one with `,` or `;` in it is written in
quotes (`font = "Times New Roman;serif"`, as LibreOffice names some). The
language server completes fonts: those of the document first, then those
installed (from fontconfig); any word of a name finds it (`roman`, `new rom`:
Times New Roman). Right after `font = ` your completion menu may need asking
for (<C-Space> in blink.cmp), or a letter typed.

Colour goes the same way, `color` (or `colour`): a few words in a span
(`[Important]{color = red}`), the paragraphs under a format's line
(`{color = darkblue}+`, `none` for their style's), a table cell's text
(`| {color = red} ... |`), a style at the top (`Normal: {..., color = darkred}`).
A colour is a name (red, blue, darkblue, darkgreen, darkred, darkgray,
lightgray, black, white, yellow, green, cyan, magenta, and their dark ones)
or `#rrggbb`; shown by its name when it has one. Black is the colour text
has when it says none ("automatic"): it shows nothing, nor does a grey that
looks black (#222222, as text pasted from web pages has), nor a link's blue
(the link's, as its underline). A colour you delete goes back to what the
text would have without it.
Changing a format's font or size changes the text of the paragraphs under
it; writing only what changes, as for everything else. A new paragraph takes
the font of the format it is under.

They are the paragraph's own formatting (what Word and LibreOffice show for
it rather than its style's; a value its style gives already is none of its
own, and a font its style gives is unsaid). Opened, paragraphs one after another with the same format go under one
line of it; written, each gets its format, also those you add. Only what
changed is written: a value the same but written another way (`10mm` for
`1cm`) changes nothing. The language server completes the attributes, and
says when one isn't right; the write fails then, as for tables.

Breaks ~
                                                             *neoffice-breaks*
A `\` at the end of a line breaks the line there, inside its paragraph (the
paragraph goes on in the next line). `\newpage` is a page break. In .odt files
a page break comes before a paragraph, so there `\newpage` moves to the start
of its paragraph.

                                                                *neoffice-toc*
A table of contents is one line, `\tableofcontents` (a list of figures
`\listoffigures`, of tables `\listoftables`): its entries aren't text to
edit, Word and LibreOffice make them, so it is kept as the document has it.
Delete the line to remove it. Its title is its too, shown with it: also a
paragraph in the TOC heading's style right before it ("TOC Heading",
LibreOffice's "Contents Heading"). Type `\tableofcontents` on a line of its
own to add one: its entries are the headings (levels 1-3), each a link to its
heading, as Word makes one; its title "Contents", or the paragraph in the TOC
heading's style right before it. The styles of its title and entries are at
the top (|neoffice-styles|), to change how it looks. Its page numbers are filled by Word when it opens
the document (neoffice asks it to, when it writes one or the headings it lists
change), by LibreOffice when you update it (Tools > Update), and in the PDF of
|neoffice-pdf| always. A list of figures or of tables the document has is
kept; neoffice doesn't make new ones. It goes between paragraphs of the text,
not in a table cell or a box. As text, starting a paragraph, it is escaped:
`\\tableofcontents`.

                                                             *neoffice-vspace*
`\vspace{1cm}` on a line of its own is vertical space between paragraphs:
the paragraphs around it stay as they are; `\vspace` alone is 1cm. It is an empty paragraph of
exactly that height, with no space before or after it (its height in cm, mm,
in or pt). An empty paragraph like that, in any document, opens as
`\vspace{...}` too: in cm when it is a whole number of millimetres, else in
points. Change its length to change it; delete the line to take it away. A
blank line around it isn't needed. It goes between paragraphs of the text,
not in a table cell or a box; a length that isn't one stops the save. As
text, starting a paragraph, it is escaped: `\\vspace{1cm}`. It is highlighted
as `\newpage` is (NeofficeMark).

Escapes ~
                                                            *neoffice-escapes*
A `\` makes the next character plain text when it could be read as a mark:
`\*`, `\[`, `\#`, `\$`, `\_{`, `\-`, `\>`, `\|`, `\=`, `\+`, `\~`, `\\`, ...
Before any other character it is just a backslash (`C:\Users` stays as it
is), and a mark that is never closed is just text (`if a == b`). So deleting
one of a pair (a word deleted with the `**` in it) would turn the other into
characters and lose the formatting: such a mark shows as a warning, and a
save that would lose formatting so (or leave a `^{` or `_{` open, raising or
lowering all that follows) stops and says where. Put the other one back, or
escape it (`\*\*`) if the characters are what you mean. Brackets
are text (`[B]`, `[1, 2]`) unless they start a link, an image or a footnote:
only then is one written `\[` (as in `\[not a link](x)`, or `\[x]{y}`, which
would read as a span of text in another font). Text that
would read as something else is written escaped, so what you see is always
what the document has. The backslash shows faint (|hl-NonText|):

  `\#. Quantify...`     a paragraph that starts with "#." typed as text, not
                      a numbered list item (without `\` it would become one)
  `\<tab>`              a paragraph of only a tab or spaces (without `\` it
                      would look like a blank line, which separates
                      paragraphs); text typed after it needs none

Objects ~
What neoffice doesn't edit shows by name, as a field does (|neoffice-footnotes|):
`⟦chart⟧`, `⟦diagram⟧`, `⟦shape⟧`, `⟦drawing⟧`, `⟦equation⟧` (in .odt files),
`⟦object⟧`, `⟦box⟧` (a box in a table's cell, or with a table in it). They are
kept as they are; delete one to delete it. Other things without text show as
`￼`.

TABLES                                                       *neoffice-tables*

Tables are Markdown pipe tables: a line per row, `|` between cells, a line of
dashes after the first row, and `<br>` between the paragraphs of a cell. Their
columns are aligned when the table fits in 120 columns (else one space on each
side of a cell); after typing, |vim.lsp.buf.format()| aligns them again
(|neoffice-lsp|): >

  | Species | Mass (g) |
  |---|---|
  | *Podarcis* | 5.1 <br> (adults) |
<
Edit the cells' text, add a row by adding a line, delete a row by deleting its
line, and add or remove a column by adding or removing a cell in every row.
New rows and cells are formatted like the ones next to them (a row pasted:
its text as its column's in the row above, whatever the style says; a new
table's, as the text near it); those you don't touch keep their exact XML.
A cell says the font and size of its text, in braces at its start, when they
aren't what most of its table's text has (`| {size = 7pt} Some text |`):
write them to change them, delete them for its table's again (the language
server completes them). Nothing else of a paragraph's format goes in a cell. To make a new table, type one, with a blank line
before and after it. Cells merged across columns show as one cell (those rows
have fewer cells), and a line break inside a cell shows as `￼`. Such a row
keeps its merged cells: empty cells added after them (as a formatter may add
them) are left out when writing, and a cell typed with text takes a column
from the merged cell before it, so the table keeps its columns. A
non-breaking space (forms put one in cells to fill in; LibreOffice shades
it) shows as a faint `·`; type over it or delete it as any character. A table
inside a table's cell isn't shown (opening says so): it is kept as it is, the
cell's other paragraphs around it.

                                                       *neoffice-table-format*
Tables typeset themselves. Write only the cells; when you write the file, a
new table gets a look that suits a paper:

- booktabs rules: a thick line above and below, a thin one under the header
- columns as wide as their content: short ones (numbers, labels) never wrap;
  when the table doesn't fit, the widest columns give way and wrap (as
  LaTeX's tabularx)
- compact rows: little space above and below the text, and cell paragraphs
  without spacing or indentation, single-spaced
- centred on the page
- columns of numbers ("12", "4.3 ± 0.2", "< 0.001", "98 (12)") aligned right,
  their header too
- the header row repeated on every page, rows that never break across pages,
  a table of up to 20 rows kept on one page, and the paragraph just before it
  (its caption) kept with it

To change its look, put its options in braces right above the table, as
`name = value`, separated by commas (|neoffice-attributes|): >

  {width = full, height = tall}
  | Site | Samples | Mean |
  |---|---|---|
<
The options you leave out keep their defaults:

  rules      `booktabs` (default), `header` (only a line under the header),
             `rows` (lines above, below and between the rows), `grid` (every
             line), `none`
  width      `auto` (default: as narrow as the content), `full` (the width of
             the text), a percentage of it (`80%`), or a length (`12cm`,
             `80mm`, `5in`)
  height     `compact` (default), `normal`, `tall`: 0.05, 0.1 or 0.2 cm above
             and below the text of each cell
  position   `center` (default), `left`, `right`

A value alone is enough (`{full, tall}`): it says which option it is. An
option or value neoffice doesn't know is an error in the language server as
you type, and makes the write fail, saying which it knows; the file isn't
changed. Your own defaults go in |neoffice-options| (`tables = { width =
"full" }`).

The line shows how a table differs from the defaults: neoffice reads it from
the table (its lines, width, position and cell padding), so tables from
anyone show theirs (pandoc's tables, for one, show `{rules = header,
position = left}`). A table
neoffice typeset is typeset again whenever it changes (a row added, a cell
edited): its widths and rules follow. Anyone else's table is left as it is
until you change its line; then neoffice typesets it. Deleting the line goes
back to the defaults.

Colons in the line of dashes align a column by hand, as in Markdown:
`|:---:|` centered, `|---:|` right-aligned, `|:---|` left. Every paragraph of
the column takes it, also those of rows you add, and it wins over the
automatic alignment of numbers; a cell can still have its own alignment mark
(|neoffice-align|). A column shows its alignment there when all its cells
have it; otherwise each cell shows its own.

==============================================================================
FOOTNOTES AND FIELDS                                      *neoffice-footnotes*

A footnote is `^[its text]`, where its number is in the text: edit its text,
type a new one, or delete it (in .docx files it leaves footnotes.xml too). A
new footnote looks like the document's other footnotes. One with several
paragraphs shows them joined; editing it makes it one paragraph. Endnotes show
as `￼`.

                                                             *neoffice-fields*
Fields (citations from Zotero or Mendeley, cross-references, dates, page
numbers, ...) show their text between `⟦ ⟧`. Their text can't be edited, as
Word or Zotero would overwrite it: writing a document with an edited field
fails, says what the field is, and changes nothing (`u` undoes the edit). Delete the whole `⟦...⟧` to delete it.

Zotero's citations in .odt files (reference marks) and in .docx files (fields)
are fields. A line typed again (`cc`) or a paragraph moved (`dd`, `p`) keeps
its citations: a `⟦...⟧` with a deleted citation's text is that citation. One
typed by hand, or a second copy, isn't a field: writing fails and says so
(`\⟦` is the character itself). Add citations with Zotero.

                                                         *neoffice-attributes*
Attributes in braces, a table's options and an image's width, are written
`name = value`, separated by commas: `{rules = grid, width = full}`,
`{width = 12cm}` (a highlight's colour is written alone: `=={green}`).
`name=value` (no spaces) and other separators work too; the
language server (|neoffice-lsp|) then hints the tidy form, and its code
action (|vim.lsp.buf.code_action()|) writes it so.

==============================================================================
CAPTIONS                                                   *neoffice-captions*

A table's or a figure's caption is a paragraph that starts with `Table: ` or
`Figure: ` (as pandoc writes a table's caption): >

  Table: Sprint speed of lizards at three sites.

  | Site | Lizards | Mean speed (m/s) |
  |---|---|---|
  ...

  ![A lizard on a wall](figures/lizard.png){width = 8cm}

  Figure: A lizard on a granite wall.
<
In the document it is a real caption: in the caption style ("Caption", or
pandoc's "Table Caption" and "Image Caption", LibreOffice's "Table" and
"Figure", whichever the document has), with its word, its number (a field
that counts the captions of its kind: Word's "SEQ Table", LibreOffice's
sequences, so Word and LibreOffice number them, and cross-reference them, as
their own) and a separator before the text: "Table 1. Sprint speed...". The
number shows in the buffer too, after the word, as heading numbers do; it
isn't typed. Captions are numbered again in order whenever you write.

Put a table's caption above it and a figure's below it (or as your journal
wants): a table's caption is kept on the same page as its table, and a
figure (the paragraph before its caption) with its caption. Captions are also
listed with the headings (|gO|, |neoffice-lsp|).

Captions made in Word (Insert Caption) or LibreOffice show the same way, and
keep their word ("Fig.") and separator (`:`, `.`, ` – `...) when you edit
them; one numbered by hand ("Fig. 3", no field) keeps its number. Deleting
`Table: ` makes it a plain paragraph again. The words, and the separator of
new captions, are options (|neoffice-options|): each word, or a list of them
(the first written, the others also read, as "Fig."): `captions = { table =
"Tabla", figure = { "Figura", "Fig." }, separator = ": " }`. A paragraph that
only starts like a caption is written `\Table: ...`. (A caption written in the
same paragraph as its image, after it, stays text: put it in a paragraph of
its own, `Figure: ...`, to make it a caption.)

==============================================================================
IMAGES                                                       *neoffice-images*

Images show as `![alt](path){width = 12.5cm}`, where the path is the image's
file inside the document (`word/media/image1.png`, `Pictures/...`).

- To replace one, write the path of a file on disk instead (absolute, `~/...`,
  or relative to the document's folder).
- To add one, type `![alt](file.png)` anywhere (PNG, JPEG or GIF).
- To remove one, delete its text. The alt text can be edited too.
- To resize one, change its width (the height follows): in `cm`, `mm`, `in`,
  or `%` of the text's width (the page's, without its margins: `{width =
  100%}` fills it). Opened again, the width shows in cm.

A new image gets the width you write, or else its own size, up to 6 inches; a
replaced one keeps the width of the image it replaces. When written, a new
image goes into the document and its path then shows its place inside it.

                                                                *neoffice-crop*
A cropped image says how much is cut off each side, as a percentage of the
whole image (only the sides cut show): >

  ![](word/media/image6.jpeg){width = 8.3cm, crop-top = 39.3%, crop-right = 11.3%}
<
`crop-top`, `crop-right`, `crop-bottom`, `crop-left`. Change them, add them or
delete them: Word and LibreOffice crop it so, and it keeps its width (its
height is that of what is shown). Its preview shows it cropped too (with
ImageMagick). A box can't be cropped, only its images.

                                                              *neoffice-floats*
An image out of the line, with the text around it (a "floating" one), says
where it goes in its braces, not with coordinates (as LaTeX's wrapfigure): >

  ![A burrow](figures/burrow.png){width = 6cm, float = right}
<
  `float`   `left`, `right`: at that side of the column, the text beside it;
            `center`: in the middle, the text above and below it. (Without
            it: in the line, as a character.)
  `wrap`    `square` (the default at the left or right), `tight`, `none`
            (the text above and below: the default in the middle), `front`
            or `behind` (over or under the text)
  `place`   `here` (the default: at its paragraph), `top` or `bottom` (of the
            page), `page` (at `x`, `y` from the page's corner)
  `gap`     the space between it and the text (default `0.3cm`)
  `x`, `y`  an exact place (from the column's edge and the paragraph's top),
            shown when it is none of the above

As everywhere in braces, a value alone is enough (`{6cm, right}`). Images
placed in Word or LibreOffice show what they mean: an image whose right edge
is the column's shows `float = right`; only a place that is none of these
shows numbers. A floating image shows on a line of its own, right above its
paragraph's text (after the paragraph's marks, if it has any: `<=> `), wherever
its anchor is in the text; written, it goes at the start of its paragraph (its
place is measured from its paragraph: it stays where it was on the page). A
line that holds only floating images is joined to the next with nothing
between: the text is the paragraph's as it was. In a figure, floating images
placed under its picture (a map on top, two plots under it) show on a line of
their own after it, as they are on the page, left to right; that line is
joined to the one before it with nothing. Several images on a line show side
by side. (Without `float`, an image on
its own line is in the line, and joined to the text with a space, as lines
are.) A floating image and its caption go together in a box
(|neoffice-boxes|).

==============================================================================
BOXES                                                         *neoffice-boxes*

A box (a text box, a frame) holds paragraphs and goes where its attributes
say, as a floating image does (|neoffice-floats|). It is written as pandoc's
fenced blocks: >

  ::: {width = 5.5cm, float = right}
  ![](figures/assa.png)

  Figure: Extreme temperature variation in micro-habitats of gerbils.
  :::

  Gerbils (Gerbillinae) are among the most diverse rodents...
<
What is inside is text like any other: formatting, comments, tracked changes,
captions. A new image in a box is as wide as the box unless it says. The box
goes with the paragraph after it (in Word and LibreOffice, it is anchored
there), at its place on the page; it is as tall as what it holds.

This is how a figure floats with its caption (as LaTeX's
`\begin{wrapfigure}...\caption`): an image and an ordinary paragraph after
it would go each its own way. The language server offers it: on a floating
image followed by its `Figure:` caption, the code action "Box this figure with
its caption" puts both in a box, and the image's place becomes the box's.

Boxes made in Word (text boxes) and LibreOffice (frames, and shapes with text)
show the same way. A box in a table's cell, or with a table in it, shows as
`⟦box⟧` and is kept as it is. Delete a box's lines and what is between them to
delete it. A paragraph that only starts like a box's line is written `\:::`.

With Nvim 0.12+ (|vim.ui.img|) and a terminal that shows images (Kitty,
Ghostty, WezTerm, ...), images are also shown under their line. Their previews
are made in the background, so a document opens without waiting for them: each
shows when it is ready (large photos are read at the size they are shown).
PNG needs nothing; JPEG, GIF, BMP, TIFF and WebP need ImageMagick (`magick`);
PDF (its first page) `pdftoppm` (poppler) or ImageMagick; SVG `rsvg-convert`
or ImageMagick; EMF, WMF, SVM and other drawings LibreOffice (`soffice`).

==============================================================================
EQUATIONS                                                 *neoffice-equations*

In .docx files, equations show as LaTeX: `$...$` within text, `$$...$$` on a
line of their own. Edit the LaTeX, type a new equation, or delete one;
equations you don't edit keep their exact XML. A `$` must touch the equation
(`$x$`, not `$ x $`), so `$5 and $10` stays text.

This needs pandoc, which converts between LaTeX and Word's equations. Without
it, equations show as `￼` and are kept as they are. An equation pandoc can't
convert makes writing fail, without changing the file. In .odt files formulas
show as `￼` and are kept as they are.

==============================================================================
COMMENTS                                                   *neoffice-comments*

Comments are underlined, with  in the sign column. |:Neoffice| comment
comments the selection (or the cursor position), and reply, resolve and
delete act on the comment under the cursor. To read them: the panel (below),
or |K| on commented text (|neoffice-lsp|), or each comment's author and text
at the end of its line, with :Neoffice comments text [on|off] (toggles; on
from the start with `comment_text = true`, |neoffice-options|).

                                                     *neoffice-comments-panel*
:Neoffice comments toggles the comments panel, with every comment and its
replies. It opens on the comment closest to the cursor, and follows the cursor
as you move in the document; the text of the comment it is on is highlighted
in the document (NeofficeCommentCurrent), and moving in the panel scrolls the
document to it when it is out of view. It looks as fugitive's status window:
the document it is about and its help, then the comments in two sections,
Open and Resolved (with how many), each its author and date, its text
indented under it, and its replies a step in. :w in it saves the document
(its comments are the document's). Its keys are two keys a family, as
fugitive's (each can be changed, or dropped: |neoffice-keymaps|); g? in it
opens this:

  <CR>     go to the comment under the cursor in the document
  aa       add a reply to it (in a buffer: |neoffice-comment-buffer|)
  ee       edit its text (or the reply's under the cursor), in a buffer
  rr       resolve it, or reopen it
  dd       delete it (or the reply under the cursor)
  ]] [[    go to the next / previous comment
  tt       each comment's text at the end of its line in the document, or
           not (as :Neoffice comments text)
  u <C-r>  undo / redo what you did to the comments: a reply, a resolve, a
           deletion (the comment comes back on its text), an edit, a new
           comment (they aren't the document's text: its own undo doesn't
           reach them)
  gq  q    close the panel
  g?       this help

:Neoffice edit edits the text of the comment under the cursor in the document
too.

                                                     *neoffice-comment-buffer*
A comment is written in a buffer of its own (a reply, a new comment, a
comment's text edited), as fugitive's commit message: a split across the
whole width (at the bottom, or the top: as 'splitbelow' says), the text on
top and, in lines starting with `#` under it, what it answers and what to do;
those lines are left out. As git takes a commit's message, what you write is
added to the document when the buffer closes: :wq (or :w, then :q); :q! with
nothing written leaves it, adding nothing; an empty text adds nothing, also
one written before (to change your mind after a :w, delete the text and
:wq, as with an empty commit message). u in
it undoes only what you typed. A new comment goes on the text it was asked
for, also if the document changed meanwhile. Then save the document (:w in
it, or in the panel), as for any change.

A reply is written as Word writes one: in its thread (commentsExtended.xml),
on the same text as its comment, with its own reference mark, so an
application that doesn't show threads still finds it. Comments you don't
touch keep their exact XML, also in Word's own comment parts.

==============================================================================
TRACKED CHANGES                                             *neoffice-changes*

As in Word: inserted text is underlined, deleted text stays in the text,
struck through (a deleted paragraph break shows as `¶`), and formatting
changes are underlined with dashes. Each author's changes have a colour of
their own, and with the cursor on a change the end of the line says who made
it and when (`Bob · 2024-01-02 · inserted`).

Reviewing ~
                                                     *neoffice-accept-reject*
Accept keeps what you see: inserted text stays, deleted text goes. Reject goes
back: inserted text goes, deleted text stays. Either acts on

- the change at the cursor (or the nearest one on the line),
- with a visual selection, the selected part of every change it touches (the
  rest of a change stays a change); with a block selection (|CTRL-V|), the
  part of each line in the block,
- or all of them (`all`), or all of someone's (`all Bob`).

|u| and |CTRL-R| undo and redo reviews and tracked edits, with their changes.

Tracking your edits ~
                                                       *neoffice-tracking*
With tracking on (`:Neoffice track`), what you type is a tracked insertion and
what you delete stays in the text as a tracked deletion, under your `author`
name. Tracking starts on when the document has it on; turning it on or off
only affects your session (the document's own setting is not changed).

- What is recorded is exactly what you edited, where you edited it (Nvim
  tells where each edit is): typing `is ` next to an `is` never takes the old
  letters as yours; a replacement (|cw|, |s|, pasting over a selection) is the
  old text deleted and the new one inserted; edits made at once in several
  places (`:s///g`) are each recorded where they are.
- Typing inside your own insertion extends it. What you type inside someone
  else's insertion, or inside or right before a deletion, is your own
  insertion: theirs is split around it.
- Deleting your own inserted text just removes it, and deleted text can't be
  deleted again (it stays deleted by whoever deleted it).
- With tracking off, what you type is plain text, also inside or next to
  someone's changes (they are split around it).
- Deleted text stays exactly as it was, with its formatting (written so in
  the file), and with what is anchored in it (comments, images, others'
  changes): rejecting the deletion brings it all back. (In .odt files the
  deleted text is written without formatting.)
- Images, equations, footnotes, fields and breaks can't be deleted while
  tracking (they are put back, and you are told so): turn tracking off to
  delete them. Editing an image's alt text, path or width, an equation or a
  footnote isn't tracked (Word doesn't either).
- Changing formatting, a paragraph's kind or its alignment isn't tracked.
- Rejecting a formatting change restores the old formatting in .docx. .odt
  files don't keep the formatting from before the change, so there rejecting
  only removes the mark.

==============================================================================
LANGUAGE SERVER                                                 *neoffice-lsp*

neoffice runs a small language server for documents, inside Nvim (nothing to
install), attached to each document as `neoffice_ls` (the plugin is
neoffice, and so is the documents' filetype). It answers:

- Document symbols: the title and the headings, nested by level (numbered
  when the document numbers them), each over its section. |gO| lists them,
  and so does any picker or outline that shows LSP symbols (fzf-lua's
  `lsp_document_symbols`, aerial, ...): a quick way to go to a section.
- Folding ranges: each heading's section, with LSP folding: >lua
    vim.wo.foldmethod, vim.wo.foldexpr = "expr", "v:lua.vim.lsp.foldexpr()"
<- Hover (|K|): on commented text, the comment and its replies; on a tracked
  change, who made it and when.
- Diagnostics on attributes in braces (|neoffice-attributes|): an error for a
  table option that isn't one, a hint when they aren't written `name =
  value, ...`; a code action (|vim.lsp.buf.code_action()|) rewrites them so.
- Formatting (|vim.lsp.buf.format()|, also of a range): tables aligned, with
  their line of dashes (added if missing), as neoffice shows them when it
  opens a document (a row with fewer cells keeps them: its cells are merged);
  attributes in braces tidy, and the lines at the top.
  Only the spaces and `|` between the cells' text change, and what is inside
  braces: the text keeps its formatting, comments and tracked changes.
  conform.nvim uses it with `lsp_format = "fallback"` (or `"prefer"`): >lua
    require("conform").format({ lsp_format = "fallback" })
<- Captions are document symbols too, in the section they are in.
- Code action on a floating image followed by its caption: put both in a box
  (|neoffice-boxes|).
- Completion: on a table's options line, the options not given yet with
  their values (and after `name =`, that option's values); in an image's
  braces and a box's, their attributes (|neoffice-floats|); after `=={`,
  highlight colours.
                                                           *neoffice-snippets*
- Snippets, in neoffice's markup (your completion plugin expands them, and
  jumps between their fields). At a line's start:

    `head`         the page and the paragraph styles, at the top
    `table`        a table, its caption above it and its options' line
    `figure`       an image and its caption under it
    `floatfigure`  an image and its caption in a box the text goes around
    `box`          a box (text box)
    `landscape`    landscape pages, and portrait again after them
    `section`      a section: a new page with its own settings
    `newpage`      a page break
    `vspace`       vertical space between paragraphs (|neoffice-vspace|)
    `toc`          a table of contents (|neoffice-toc|)
    `equation`     an equation of its own
    `numbered`     a numbered list, with an item under another
    `bullets`      a bulleted list
    `quote`        a quotation
    `title`        the title
    `format`       a format's line for the paragraphs after it (`+`)

  After a space: `footnote` (right after the word before it), `link`, `span`
  (words in another font or size), `highlight`, `math`.

`lsp = false` (|neoffice-options|) turns it off.

==============================================================================
PDF                                                             *neoffice-pdf*

`:Neoffice pdf` writes the document, as saved, as a PDF next to it
(report.docx gives report.pdf, in the same folder; one there is replaced) and
opens it. `:Neoffice pdf auto` writes it again after each |:w| (on, or off
again; `auto on`, `auto off` say which). A viewer that reloads a file when it
changes (zathura, Okular, Evince, sioyek, SumatraPDF, Skim...) then shows each
save as you make it, in the same window.

LibreOffice (`soffice`) makes it, with its Python bridge (`import uno`, as
Linux distributions package it: python3-uno, libreoffice-script-provider-python):
one LibreOffice, hidden, started at the first export and stopped when Nvim
quits, so an export takes a fraction of a second. Without the bridge, each
export starts LibreOffice, which takes a few seconds. Exports run in the
background: typing and saving never wait for one, and a save made while one is
being written is exported after it (the last one only).

The PDF is of the file as saved: unsaved changes aren't in it (you are told).
Neither the document nor its folder is touched: LibreOffice reads a copy in
Nvim's cache, and the PDF is written under a hidden name next to the document,
then put in place, so a viewer never shows half of one. Settings: `pdf` in
|neoffice-options|.

==============================================================================
FOR OTHER PLUGINS                                              *neoffice-read*

`require("neoffice.read")` reads documents for other plugins (manuscript.nvim
brings a revised document back into its LaTeX sources with it). It is kept
stable; its `version` field changes if it doesn't.

  read.is_document({buf})   whether {buf} is a document opened by neoffice
  read.path({buf})          its file
  read.has_changes({buf})   whether it has tracked changes
  read.paragraphs({buf})    its paragraphs (see below)
  read.file({path})         the paragraphs of a document file, read in a
                            hidden buffer (nil and the error if it can't be)
  read.diff({a}, {b})       hunks between two lists of strings

A paragraph is { first, last, text, sent, revised, comments }: its buffer
lines and text; its characters as they were before its tracked changes (all
rejected, with the formatting from before formatting changes) and with them
(all accepted), each { ch, b, i, u, s, h, sup, sub, link, ... }; and its
comments, { author, text, resolved, replies = { { author, text }, ... } }.

COMMANDS                                                         *:Neoffice*

Everything is a subcommand of :Neoffice. Alone, it lets you pick one.

:Neoffice comments              Toggle the comments panel.
:Neoffice comments text [on|off]
                                Show each comment's author and text at the
                                end of its line, or not (toggles).
:Neoffice comment               Comment the selection, or the cursor position.
:Neoffice reply                 Reply to the comment under the cursor.
:Neoffice resolve               Resolve (or reopen) the comment under the
                                cursor.
:Neoffice delete                Delete the comment under the cursor.
:Neoffice track [on|off]        Record your edits as tracked changes
                                (toggles).
:Neoffice accept [all [{author}]]
                                Accept the change at the cursor, the selected
                                part, all, or all of {author}'s.
:Neoffice reject [all [{author}]]
                                Reject them, the same way.
:Neoffice next                  Jump to the next tracked change.
:Neoffice prev                  Jump to the previous tracked change.
:Neoffice changes [{author}]    List the tracked changes (of {author}) in the
                                |location-list|.
:Neoffice numbers [on|off]      Number the headings 1, 1.1, 1.2, 2, ... as
                                Word and LibreOffice do it, through the
                                heading styles (toggles). The numbers are
                                shown before the headings, but aren't part of
                                the text.
:Neoffice pdf                   Write the document, as saved, as a PDF next
                                to it, and open it (|neoffice-pdf|).
:Neoffice pdf auto [on|off]     Write it again after each save (toggles).
:Neoffice styles reset          Make the styles a new document's
                                (|neoffice-styles-reset|).

<Tab> completes the subcommands and the authors' names.

KEYMAPS                                                     *neoffice-keymaps*

neoffice sets one key in documents: g? opens this help (across the whole
width, at the bottom or the top as 'splitbelow' says). The comments panel has
its own (|neoffice-comments-panel|), and g? there opens its part of this help.
Each can be changed, or dropped with false (all of them: `keymaps = false`): >lua

  require("neoffice").setup({
    keymaps = {
      help = "g?",
      comments = {
        go = "<CR>", reply = "aa", edit = "ee", resolve = "rr", delete = "dd",
        text = "tt", next = "]]", prev = "[[", undo = "u", redo = "<C-r>",
        close = { "gq", "q" }, help = "g?",
      },

  vim.api.nvim_create_autocmd("FileType", {
    pattern = "neoffice",

      local function map(lhs, cmd, desc, mode)

      map("e", "edit", "Edit comment")
      map("x", "resolve", "Resolve / reopen comment")
      map("d", "delete", "Delete comment")
      map("t", "track", "Track changes on/off")
      map("j", "next", "Next change")
      map("k", "prev", "Previous change")
      map("y", "accept", "Accept change", { "n", "x" })
      map("n", "reject", "Reject change", { "n", "x" })
      map("Y", "accept all", "Accept all changes")
      map("N", "reject all", "Reject all changes")
      map("l", "changes", "List changes")
      map("h", "numbers", "Heading numbers on/off")

OPTIONS                                                     *neoffice-options*

                                                            *neoffice.setup()*
setup() is optional. Every field is optional too; these are the defaults: >lua

  require("neoffice").setup({
    author = vim.env.USER, -- on your comments and tracked changes
    track_changes = nil,   -- true/false: always/never track your edits;
                           -- nil: as the document says
    conceal = false,       -- hide the marks (except on the cursor line)
    styles = true,         -- the styles at the top (|neoffice-styles|)
    page = true,           -- the page's settings and sections (|neoffice-pages|)
    show_tabs = true,      -- show tabs as a faint →, non-breaking spaces as a faint · (|'list'|, in its windows only;
                           -- the Tab key types a tab, whatever 'expandtab' says)
    images = true,         -- show images (where the terminal can)
    lsp = true,            -- the language server (|neoffice-lsp|)
    comment_text = false,  -- each comment's text at the end of its line
    doc_cache = nil,       -- where .doc files converted by LibreOffice are kept, so
                           -- one opens at once the next time (nil: Neovim's cache
                           -- directory; false: converted each time)
    pdf = {                -- PDF exports (|neoffice-pdf|)
      auto = false,        -- after each save, in every document (:Neoffice pdf
                           -- auto turns it on or off in one)
      open = true,         -- open the PDF the first time it is written
      viewer = nil,        -- the program to open it with ("zathura", or
                           -- { "okular", "--unique" }); nil: the system's
      quiet = false,       -- no message when one is written
      timeout = 120,       -- seconds before an export is given up
    },
    image_width = 60,      -- at most this many columns wide
    image_height = 20,     -- and this many lines high
    captions = {           -- their words, and what goes after the number
      table = "Table", figure = { "Figure", "Fig." }, separator = ". ",
    },
    tables = {             -- how tables look (|neoffice-table-format|)
      rules = "booktabs", width = "auto", height = "compact", position = "center",
    },
    keymaps = {            -- neoffice's keys (|neoffice-keymaps|; false drops one,
      help = "g?",         -- keymaps = false all): in documents, its help;
      comments = {         -- in the comments panel
        go = "<CR>", reply = "aa", edit = "ee", resolve = "rr", delete = "dd",
        text = "tt", next = "]]", prev = "[[", undo = "u", redo = "<C-r>",
        close = { "gq", "q" }, help = "g?",
      },
    },
  })
<
                                                         *neoffice-highlights*
The colours say what is what: text in the text's colour; formatting in the
text (`**`, `==`, `\`, tabs as `→`, alignment marks, attributes in braces) in
one gray, quieter than the text but readable (NeofficeMark: made from your
colour scheme, half way between its text and background colours, again when it
changes); what says what a paragraph is in that element's colour (a heading's
`#`, a title's `%`, a list's `-`, a quote's `>`, a caption's word, a table's
`|`, a box's `:::`); a link or an image, all of it (`[text](url)`,
`![alt](path)`), as your scheme shows links; fields and numbers as your scheme
shows special things; comments and changes in their own colours. Your scheme's
colours for markdown (headings, links, code) are a document's too.

Highlight groups (all `default`, so your colour scheme can set them):

  NeofficeMark              formatting in the text: marks, attributes, `\`,
                            tabs (NeofficeAlign, NeofficeEscape,
                            NeofficeTableOptions link to it)
  NeofficeBox               a box's `:::` lines
  NeofficeEscape            a `\` before a character that is just text
  NeofficeInsert1 ... 6     insertions, one colour per author
  NeofficeDelete1 ... 6     deletions, one colour per author
  NeofficeChangeInfo        who made the change at the cursor, and when
  NeofficeFormat            formatting changes
  NeofficeComment           commented text
  NeofficeCommentSign       the comment sign
  NeofficeCommentVirt       a comment's text at the end of its line
  NeofficeCommentCurrent    the text of the comment the panel is on
  The comments panel and a comment's buffer are highlighted by their syntax
  files (syntax/neoffice-comments.vim, syntax/neoffice-comment.vim), as
  fugitive's: neofficeCommentsHeader (Label), neofficeCommentsHelpTag (Tag),
  neofficeCommentsHeading (PreProc), neofficeCommentsCount (Number),
  neofficeCommentsAuthor (Identifier), neofficeCommentsDate (Comment),

WHAT IS (AND ISN'T) CHANGED                                  *neoffice-safety*

- Writing without changes writes nothing: the file stays byte for byte as it
  was.
- Only the paragraphs you edited are rewritten. The others, and every part of
  the file you didn't touch (styles, headers, footnotes, settings, ...), are
  written back exactly as they were.
- Writing is all or nothing: if something is wrong (an image file that doesn't
  exist, an equation that can't be converted), the file is not changed and the
  buffer stays modified. The file is replaced in one step, so it is never left
  half written.
- Every part about to be written is checked first (one root element, every
  prefix declared): a part Word or LibreOffice couldn't read is never written;
  the save fails and says so (that would be a bug in neoffice).
- If another program changed the file since it was read or written, writing
  stops; |:w!| writes over it.
- A document that can't be read opens read-only and empty, so it can't be
  written over.
- `:w other.docx` writes a copy and leaves the original alone; |:saveas|
  makes the new file the document's. A document can't be written in another
  format (`:w copy.odt` from a .docx is refused).
- In paragraphs you edit, Word's spell-check marks (`w:proofErr`) are dropped
  (Word recreates them).
- .doc files go through LibreOffice's converter, so what it can't convert is
  lost.
